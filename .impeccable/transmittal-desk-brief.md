## Direction contract

**THESIS:** A request is a transmittal: a clear handoff from a person with a need to the team accountable for its progress. The UI refuses generic SaaS cards and decorative dashboard clutter.

**OWN-WORLD:** Matte warm-paper ground, charcoal-green ink, pale mist-blue annotations, restrained brass service markers, and fine registrar rules. The type pairing is a high-legibility workhorse sans with Unna only for large task statements. Surfaces are defined by lines and measured spacing, never broad shadows.

**STORY:** A requester immediately chooses the right path, understands what happens after submission, and can track a ticket without seeing technical data. An engineer moves from the same handoff language into a dense, fast register of work.

**FIRST VIEWPORT:** A slim document header sits above a two-column desktop handoff. The left column carries the operational promise, task statement, and a quiet blueprint plate. The right column is a numbered vertical register of three request paths; each row has a brass marker, direct description, and forward action. On mobile the statement, register, and tracking action form one vertical sequence.
